# Character and art direction
Proposed design, subject to approval: recurring adult Indian woman storyteller, warm approachable expression, elegant simple sari or kurta, natural brown skin tones, hand-drawn Indian storybook animation style (not photorealistic or yellow-tinted). A calm courtyard/library with open brass deepams and traditional Indian design. No stereotyped exaggeration.

Create a character sheet with front, three-quarter and side views, neutral, welcoming and reflective expressions. Maintain facial features, proportions, clothes and palette across shots. Separate eyes, mouth and limbs only if useful for gentle movement. No continuous lip-sync required; avoid obviously mismatched mouth motion.

For Tulasi, Shankhachuda, Vishnu and Shiva, research the relevant devotional iconography and keep designs consistent. Do not invent specific canonical costume details when unsupported. Shankhachuda should not be reduced to a generic monster. Tulasi should retain agency and dignity. Depict the disguise without sexualized imagery. Label sacred-story artwork as artistic interpretation.

Palette: emerald, muted terracotta, warm ivory, marigold gold, natural skin tones. Use varied scene composition rather than repetitive oil paintings. Preserve authentic Tulsi plant, Shaligram, sugarcane and open oil lamps. No text inside generated images.
