Why It Matters

The Sacred Significance

The Hindu New Year — Chaitra Shukla Pratipada — is not a social convention but a cosmic event. It marks the alignment of lunar, solar and planetary cycles, rooted in Vedic astronomy refined over 5,000 years. Unlike any other calendar, the Hindu Panchang tracks five simultaneous dimensions of time.

🌌

Creation of the Universe

Mythologically, Lord Brahma began creating the universe on this very day — Chaitra Shukla Pratipada — making it the most primordial of all beginnings in Hindu cosmology.

👑

Victory of Vikramaditya

The Vikram Samvat era began around 57 BCE when the legendary Emperor Vikramaditya of Ujjain defeated the Shaka invaders, establishing a calendar of cultural triumph.

🌸

Vasant — Spring Renewal

The new year begins as spring arrives, symbolising the renewal of life. Nature herself celebrates: flowers bloom, crops grow, and the world is reborn in colour and fragrance.

🌞

Solar-Lunar Harmony

The Vikram Samvat is a lunisolar calendar — it tracks both moon phases (for religious dates) and solar movement (for seasons), keeping festivals aligned with nature perpetually.

🏹

Ram Rajyabhishek

Traditional belief holds that Lord Rama's coronation after his return from Lanka also took place during Chaitra, adding royal and dharmic significance to this month's new year.

📖

Agricultural Cycle

The new year marks the end of one harvest and the beginning of another sowing cycle. Farmers, merchants, and communities all recalibrate their year around this anchor date.

🔁

Adhik Maas in 2083

This year (Vikram Samvat 2083) features a rare 13th lunar month — Adhik Jyeshtha — occurring 17 May to 15 June 2026. Lord Vishnu accepted it as Purushottam Maas, making it highly spiritually meritorious.

♃

Roudra Samvatsara

The year 2083 is named Roudra Samvatsara. Jupiter is the year's king (wisdom, dharma, prosperity) while Mars serves as minister (courage, valour), promising a year of righteousness and determination.

One Spirit, Many Names

Regional Celebrations

The same cosmic event is celebrated across India under different names — each region adding its own poetry, colour and ritual to the universal moment of renewal.

🚩

Gudi Padwa

Maharashtra • Chaitra 1

A decorated Gudi (bamboo staff with a bright cloth, neem leaves, sugar crystals and a pot) is hoisted outside homes to symbolise victory and prosperity. Families prepare traditional Puran Poli and draw rangoli. The Gudi is believed to ward off evil and invite Brahma's blessings into the home.

🌿

Ugadi

Andhra Pradesh & Telangana • Chaitra 1

Ugadi means "beginning of an era" (Yuga + Adi). The unique ritual is eating Ugadi Pachadi — a chutney with six tastes (sweet, sour, bitter, spicy, astringent, salty) representing all the flavours life will bring in the coming year. The Panchanga Sravanam (almanac reading) is performed at temples.

🌾

Yugadi

Karnataka • Chaitra 1

Virtually identical to Ugadi in spirit, Karnataka's Yugadi is celebrated with temple visits, homes adorned with mango leaves (torana), and the preparation of the same symbolic six-taste pachadi. Communities gather for the Panchanga Shravana where the year's predictions are read aloud by priests.

💡

Bestu Varas / Vikram Samvat New Year

Gujarat • Kartik 1 (day after Diwali)

Uniquely, Gujarat celebrates its new year (Vikram Samvat) on the day after Diwali in October/November, not in Chaitra. It's called Bestu Varas. Merchants open new accounting books in a ritual called Chopda Pujan. The greeting "Saal Mubarak" rings through communities worldwide. The day is a public holiday in Gujarat.

🌻

Vaisakhi / Baisakhi

Punjab & North India • Vaishakha 1 (13-14 April)

Vaisakhi marks the Solar New Year in Punjab, celebrating the wheat harvest. It also commemorates the founding of the Khalsa Panth by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699. Grand processions, bhangra dancing, and community langar (free communal meals) mark the celebrations. It's among the most energetic new year festivals in the subcontinent.

🪔

Hindu Nav Varsh / Nav Samvat

North & Central India • Chaitra 1

Observed across UP, Rajasthan, MP, Bihar and Delhi as Hindu Nav Varsh with temple visits, Panchang readings, and the beginning of Chaitra Navratri — nine sacred nights devoted to Goddess Durga. Many families begin the day with an early bath and prayers, setting intentions for the year ahead.

❄️

Navreh

Kashmir • Chaitra 1 (Kashmiri Pandits)

Navreh is the Kashmiri Pandit new year, observed on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada. A thaal (plate) containing rice, walnuts, salt, coins, a pen, curd, flowers and a Panchanga is prepared the previous night and viewed first thing in the morning — the items symbolise abundance, wisdom and good fortune for the year ahead.

🏔️

Nepali New Year (Nava Barsha)

Nepal • Baisakh 1 (mid-April)

Nepal uses the Bikram Sambat as its official national civil calendar. The new year begins with Baisakh 1 (around 13-15 April), which is a national public holiday. The Bisket Jatra carnival in Bhaktapur is famous for its chariot procession. Nepal's year count is 57 ahead of the Gregorian calendar.

The Cosmic Calendar

The 12 Sacred Months

Each Hindu month is named after the nakshatra (constellation) in which the full moon rises during that month — encoding ancient astronomical observations into everyday timekeeping. Each month carries its own season, deity, and spiritual character.

Five Limbs of Time

The Panchang — पञ्चाङ्ग

The Hindu calendar is not a single number but a five-dimensional system called the Panchang (from pancha = five, anga = limb). Every date is defined by five simultaneous values — which is why Hindu dates shift relative to the Gregorian calendar each year.

🌙

Tithi

तिथि

Lunar day (1–30). 15 tithis in the bright fortnight (Shukla Paksha) and 15 in the dark (Krishna Paksha). The most fundamental unit of Hindu time.

📅

Vara

वार

Day of the week. Each day is ruled by a planet: Sunday (Sun), Monday (Moon), Tuesday (Mars), Wednesday (Mercury), Thursday (Jupiter), Friday (Venus), Saturday (Saturn).

⭐

Nakshatra

नक्षत्र

Lunar mansion. The Moon passes through 27 nakshatras over a month. The nakshatra at birth profoundly shapes one's astrological chart (Janma Nakshatra).

♐

Yoga

योग

Auspiciousness index. Calculated from the combined longitude of the Sun and Moon. There are 27 yogas, ranging from highly auspicious (Siddhi) to inauspicious (Vyatipata).

⏱️

Karana

करण

Half a tithi (approx. 6 hours). There are 11 karanas (4 fixed + 7 repeating). Used in muhurta calculations to find the best moment within a day for any activity.

Paksha — The Two Fortnights

🌕

Shukla Paksha

शुक्ल पक्ष

Bright (waxing) fortnight. New moon → Full moon. Considered auspicious for new beginnings, marriages, travel, and major events.

🌑

Krishna Paksha

कृष्ण पक्ष

Dark (waning) fortnight. Full moon → New moon. Often reserved for ancestor rituals (Pitru Paksha) and introspective practices.

Discover Your Hindu Date

Hindu Birthday Calculator

Your Gregorian birth date corresponds to a specific Tithi, Month, Paksha, and Samvat year in the Hindu calendar. Below, you can look up your approximate Hindu birth date — or anyone else's.

1

The Year (Samvat)

Add 57 to your Gregorian birth year for dates in April–December, or add 56 for January–March (before Hindu New Year). Example: Born 15 Aug 1990 → Samvat 1990 + 57 = 2047.

2

Find the Hindu Month

The lunar month begins roughly 15 days before mid-month. Chaitra ≈ mid-March to mid-April; Vaishakha ≈ mid-April to mid-May, and so on. The calculator below approximates your month from your Gregorian date.

3

Paksha & Tithi

The month splits into two fortnights (Paksha) of 15 lunar days (tithis) each. The exact tithi requires knowing the Moon's longitude on your birth date — use the calculator for an approximation, or consult a printed Panchang for precision.

4

For Precision — Use a Panchang

A printed or online Panchang (such as Drik Panchang at drikpanchang.com) gives the exact Tithi, Nakshatra, and Yoga for any birth date and time. The calculator here provides a reliable approximation.
