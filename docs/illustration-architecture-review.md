# Illustration setting and architecture review

Reviewed 5 November 2026 for the Dhanteras and Diwali guide pair after the owner flagged the architectural vocabulary.

## Editorial finding

The broad phrase “Indian miniature-inspired” did not constrain architecture sufficiently. Several images acquired a generic later court-palace vocabulary: domed pavilions, ornate cusped portals and decorative arcades, including in imagined epic settings. Those backdrops were not supported by a story-specific architectural brief and needed correction.

A building's religion cannot be reliably inferred from one arch shape. Hindu temple shrine frames, ritual canopies and regional domestic architecture should not be rejected simply because they contain curves or share artistic vocabulary with other traditions. The review distinguishes narrative/region fit from unsupported religious labeling.

## Targeted revisions

| Illustration | Problem addressed | Revised direction |
|---|---|---|
| Rama's return | Generic later palace gateway, domed skyline and cusped galleries | Imagined timber/stone city, straight post-and-lintel gateways, pillared verandas and distant North Indian temple shikharas; no claim to reconstruct ancient Ayodhya |
| Krishna and Narakasura | Generic domed court-fort | Imagined hill fort with masonry ramparts, timber buildings, pitched roofs and a rectangular gateway; no imposed temple in the asura city |
| Hima household folklore | Palace portal and distant domed pavilion unrelated to the household tale | Timber-and-stone veranda, straight lintels, rectangular doors and simple pitched garden canopy |
| Yama and Yamuna | Generic domed river pavilions | Open ritual mandapa, stone pillars, straight lintels and regional temple forms beyond the river steps |
| Dhanteras vessels | Craft shop rendered as an elaborate palace | Vernacular timber craft shop, rectangular opening, shelves and lime-plaster walls |
| Regional triptych | A repeated architectural arch imposed across three distinct regional scenes | Straight rectangular panels and botanical ornament; Bengali pandal, South Indian courtyard and Maharashtrian home retain their separate settings |

Original paintings are retained as superseded assets. Active article, homepage, overview-card and social-preview references point to versioned revised images. The asset provenance record preserves the previous prompt/path/hash alongside each revision.

## Retained artwork

Samudra Manthan, Dhanvantari and Govardhan primarily use cosmic/ocean/landscape settings. The household Yama lamp and family-puja scene use domestic contexts. Lakshmi, Annakut and Vamana/Bali include devotional shrine frames or ritual-pavilion elements; these are not identified as mosques or as exclusively belonging to another faith. These eight images do not require a replacement merely on the basis of a curved decorative element.

## Visual reference basis

- [Archaeological Survey of India, Temple Survey Project — temple components and regional styles](https://tspasibhopal.nic.in/temple/about_the_temple.html): mandapa, curvilinear northern shikhara and its crowning forms. A vocabulary reference, not evidence for the date or appearance of an epic city.
- [National Crafts Museum — vernacular architecture and craft context](https://nationalcraftsmuseum.nic.in/about-us): domestic materials, carved craftwork and regional vernacular settings.
- [India Cine Hub — Vishrambag Wada](https://indiacinehub.gov.in/location/vishrambag-wada): a documented Maratha wooden domestic/court building reference, used for regional craft/home vocabulary only, not as a template for an ancient epic setting.

## Review limits

These are AI-assisted interpretive devotional paintings. Architectural corrections do not make them archaeological reconstructions or establish the historical dating of religious narratives. Character identity, ritual context and regional details still need editorial care. No religion is associated with negative qualities, and no building is classified solely by its arch or roof shape.
