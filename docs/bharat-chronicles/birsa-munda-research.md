# Birsa Munda · Research and editorial notes

Reviewed 16 November 2026. This record accompanies the bilingual biography. It distinguishes direct consultation from works found through catalogues or citations. The article uses original wording and quotes no reconstructed speeches or testimony.

## Artwork provenance and briefs

- **Hero portrait.** Unknown photographer, reproduced in Roy, *The Mundas and Their Country* (1912), p. 72, where the plate names Birsa Munda. Compared the plate with the derivative attributed through the archived Sussex research page. The [Commons file record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Birsa_Munda,_photograph_in_Roy_(1912-72).JPG) carries a Public Domain Mark; the 1912 publication also predates 1931 in the United States. The archived scan is self-hosted at its native dimensions, without restoration or a fabricated date. Rycroft discusses alternative datings in the Sussex article and subsequent research. The social card places this photograph beside the separately generated plateau illustration, rather than compositing him into the landscape.
- **Chotanagpur landscape.** Generated for this article using OpenAI ImageGen and optimized as WebP. The brief specified a painterly general impression of forest cover, lateritic soil, rocky ridges and cultivated lowlands of the plateau. No human figures, settlement details, specific dated location, ceremony or map.
- **Munda village (childhood).** AI-assisted editorial painting. The [1961 Jojohatu village survey](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/31015/study-description), especially its description of clay-plastered walls, tiled roofs, verandas and simple exterior work areas, provides an architectural reference. The illustration is a composite and does not identify a house as Birsa’s or use a later survey as direct evidence for an 1870s scene.
- **Landholding and forest life.** The plateau study shows the relationship between upland woods and fields. The glossary and the three-step land rights graphic separately explain tenure and legal categories.
- **Chalkad gathering (becoming Dharti Aba).** AI-assisted editorial painting informed by Bara and Chandra on Birsa’s emergence as a teacher. The figures and composition are interpretive, with no reconstruction of a documented meeting, specific speech, ritual or attire.
- **Ulgulan at a glance.** The sequence under Dombari Buru replaces the anonymous hill illustration. It visualises four broad moments described by Chandra and the ICHR biography, from the onset in late December through suppression in early 1900. The expanded chronology remains in the interactive timeline.
- **Archival photograph (arrest).** The group photograph’s printed caption identifies Birsa as a captive taken to Ranchi. It is reproduced from Roy’s *The Mundas and Their Country* (1912). The [Commons record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Birsa_Munda_captured_and_conducted_to_Ranchi.jpg) identifies the photographer as unknown and marks the photo public domain. The full plate and original caption are preserved in the WebP; the page does not use the uploader’s exact date to resolve the historic arrest-date dispute.
- **Kesana village statue (historical memory).** [Photographed in 2019 by R Ashwani Banjan Murmu](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Birsa_Munda.jpg), licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Resized to 720 × 960 and encoded as WebP, with no crop. This is contemporary documentary evidence of commemoration. The resized photo remains under the same license.
- **Land-rights legacy.** Implemented as a sequence of customary claims, survey and settlement, and the 1908 statute. Keeps the law's scope in the text. No land parcel map or universal claim about title.
- **Petition vignette.** This AI-assisted detail of hands passing a folded sheet accompanies the Sardari Larai paragraph. It evokes the collective work of petitioning described by Bara and Chandra. The paper is blank where visible, and the composition represents no specific archival document, person or meeting.
- **Threshold vignette.** This AI-assisted picture of an empty doorway accompanies the account of Birsa’s death in custody. Its architecture and objects are symbolic, with no suggestion of a cause of death or a reconstruction of Ranchi Jail.
- **Closing tribute.** Future brief. Consider a photograph of landscape or a locally identified memorial from a visit with permission. Keep the reflection clearly editorial and avoid substituting a staged image for a community's account.
