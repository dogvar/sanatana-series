# Chhath Puja 2026 · Research and editorial notes

Reviewed 16 November 2026. These notes distinguish sourced practices, devotional accounts and the guide’s own reflections.

| Claim | Evidence and editorial choice |
| --- | --- |
| Four-day autumn observance | [Drik Panchang, 2026 Chhath calendar](https://www.drikpanchang.com/chhath/chhath-puja-calendar.html?lang=en&year=2026) explicitly lists 13 November Nahay Khay (Chaturthi), 14 November Kharna/Lohanda (Panchami), 15 November Sandhya Arghya (Shashthi), and 16 November Usha Arghya and Parana (Saptami). The date table on p. 20 of [Kolkata Municipal Corporation’s 2026 booklet](https://www.kmcgov.in/KMCPortal/downloads/Chhat_Puja_2026_English_Book.pdf) independently lists the same sequence. The date table, rather than conflicting timing phrasing elsewhere in that booklet, is used for the Kolkata cross-check. |
| Place and clock time | Drik Panchang’s default city is New Delhi (IST, UTC+5:30), and the site labels the published times local. Sunday’s arghya follows local sunset, Monday’s follows local sunrise. A Netherlands reader should choose their city and use local CET (UTC+1 in November). No clock times are printed. The direct Amsterdam locality query was blocked by the source during the automated fetch. [Amsterdam independent monthly listings](https://desi.net/amsterdam/calendar?m=2026-11) and [Amsterdam monthly panchang](https://www.astrolal.com/panchang/netherlands/amsterdam/month/2026-11) corroborate the main day on 15 November. The site calendar already had that entry, so the change adds guide links and leaves its stored date intact. |
| Devotion and ritual | [Bihar Tourism](https://tourism.bihar.gov.in/en/experiences/festivals-and-fairs/festivals/chhath-puja), [Bihar Bhawan 2024](https://www.biharbhawan.gov.in/docs/eMagazine/Newsletter_Dec_2024.pdf) and the [Sangeet Natak Akademi element brief](https://sangeetnatak.gov.in/public/uploads/events/17936931651287.pdf) describe worship of Surya and Chhathi Maiya, the vow and the day sequence. Mother Shashthi is a devotional identification. The guide does not present one genealogy for Chhathi Maiya as universal. Abstinence is described as a practice of some observers, with no prescriptive duration or purported health benefit. |
| Food and offerings | [Bihar Bhawan 2025](https://biharbhawan.gov.in/docs/eMagazine/Newsletter_Dec_2025.pdf) explains thekua and Kharna kheer. [YCMOU course material](https://ycmou.ac.in/media/publication/ycmou_book/RVV_YB_128.pdf) and [DD News reporting from the 2026 observance](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/chhath-puja-devotees-prepare-to-observe-kharna-ritual-on-second-day/) describe rice, chana dal and a gourd preparation. The guide illustrates lauki as one example. [Bharti Rai, Indi@logs 13(2), pp. 13–33](https://revistes.uab.cat/indialogs/article/download/v13-n2-rai/414-pdf-en/2973) documents bamboo baskets, offerings and water or milk for arghya. [Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/chhath-puja) describes Kosi, including sugarcane, lamps and prasad. |
| Sacred narratives | [Devi Bhagavata Purana 9.46](https://eternalraga.com/en/scriptures/puranas/devi-bhagavatam/9/46) supplies the narrative details about Kashyapa’s yajna, Priyavrata’s grief and Shashthi restoring the child to life. [Ganguli’s Vana Parva 307](https://versesacross.com/read/mahabharata/mbh3/307?v=ganguli) describes Karna’s midday prayer and Indra’s request. The river arghya belongs to the festival retelling and is distinct from the joined palms in the translated episode.  [Sahapedia’s interview-based account](https://www.sahapedia.org/chhath-puja-origin-of-the-festival-and-the-story-of-devasena) documents Devasena/Shashthi and the Priyavrata story, plus associations with Karna and Draupadi. In [Ganguli’s translation of Vana Parva III](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-mahabharata-book-3-vana-parva/section-iii), Dhaumya instructs Yudhishthira, who worships Surya and receives the vessel tied to Draupadi’s meal. The guide distinguishes that episode from Chhath stories naming Draupadi as the observer. It uses no unverified liturgical verse, historical founding date or date for an epic event. The sunset/sunrise symbolism is labeled editorial interpretation. |
| Geography and migration | [Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/chhath-puja) covers Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh. [Associated Press on-site reporting](https://apnews.com/article/21c03117b6906667a4a7d2654dd429d1) supports Jharkhand and migration within India. The [Nepal Tourism Board](https://ntb.gov.np/en/chhat-parva) names Janakpur and the Bagmati in Kathmandu. [Hayden Seth Kantor, Cornell dissertation, ch. 6](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstreams/fc820cb0-dc63-4564-8461-3690c3947e1f/download) and Bharti Rai discuss family work, song and regional identity. The guide gives local planning advice without claiming a particular Netherlands event or venue. |
| Long orange sindoor | [Harsh Aditya’s firsthand family account, Museum of Material Memory](https://museumofmaterialmemory.com/rajkali-devi-kamla-and-the-silver-sindoor-daani/) describes bright orange sindoor reaching the tip of the nose. [Kantor’s fieldwork, p. 159](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstreams/fc820cb0-dc63-4564-8461-3690c3947e1f/download) independently observes the nose-to-hair application at a Bihar ghat. A [regional report from Bhagalpur](https://www.jagran.com/bihar/bhagalpur-chhath-puja-bhagalpur-ganga-ghats-adorned-with-sindoor-sunset-glow-40437053.html) corroborates that the custom is still visible. It is illustrated and discussed as a locally recognised custom among some married women. The sources’ health and universality claims were not adopted. |

## Illustration treatment

The guide uses nine AI-assisted illustrations and two owner-supplied riverside scenes. Captions and alt text are localized. The hero shows the vrati standing in the river for Sandhya Arghya, a practice described by [Bihar Bhawan](https://www.biharbhawan.gov.in/docs/eMagazine//Newsletter_Nov_2023.pdf) and documented in [Bihar Tourism’s festival brochure](https://tourism.bihar.gov.in/content/dam/bihar-tourism/e_brochures/magnificient_bihar/magnificent_bihar_english_2020.pdf). The third-day plate shows a family arranging prasad on the ghat before the offering. Day one and day two show different prepared foods. Day four and the closing picture shift to a cooler, softer dawn. The offerings plate shows recognizable objects separately, and the additional close portrait makes the orange line from the hair parting to the nose visible. The illustrations are interpretive and identify no real participant, specific historical event or mandatory ritual arrangement. Three manuscript-style illustrations accompany the sacred stories: Shashthi with the child she revives, Karna at his midday prayer, and Draupadi serving forest guests. The social JPEG is a crop of the hero, with no embedded type.
