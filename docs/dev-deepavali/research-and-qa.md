## Source checks and editorial choices

- [Drik Panchang, Varanasi, 2026](https://www.drikpanchang.com/diwali/dev-diwali/dev-deepawali-date-time.html?geoname-id=1253405&year=2026), reviewed 24 November 2026: Tuesday 24 November. Pradoshakal 17:08–19:47; Purnima begins 23 November 23:42 and ends 24 November 20:23. Its notes specify Varanasi local time; November IST is UTC+05:30. These appear only in an optional disclosure, labelled local panchang times, not event/aarti schedules. No automatic reader-location adjustment is claimed. A city-selecting panchang link is provided.
- [Incredible India / Ministry of Tourism festival listing](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/uttar-pradesh/illuminating-the-eternal-spirit-of-kashi) independently lists 24 November 2026. Describes Kartik Purnima, traditional gods-at-Ganga belief, riverfront lamps, music and Ganga Aarti. Direct page fetch initially timed out; the search-indexed official page text and its essential-information date were retrieved. No exact lamp/crowd counts or 2026 performance schedule are adopted.
- [Akashvani report, 24 November 2026](https://newsonair.gov.in/uttar-pradesh-varanasi-gears-up-for-grand-dev-deepawali-celebrations/) corroborates Kartik Purnima bathing and evening lamp preparations. Bathing is described as customary for some devotees, not mandatory for all. Practical reflection and the closing blessing are editorial, not attributed scripture.
- Shiva Purana, J. L. Shastri translation, Rudra-samhita, Yuddha-khanda: [chapter 1](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226142.html), especially 7–9 and 25–59, supports the three brothers, boon, cities and alignment condition. Follow the chapter body (gold, silver, iron/steel), not its inconsistent introductory footnote mentioning copper. The guide does not combine alternative builders or versions from other retellings.
- [Chapter 2](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226143.html), 3–12, supports the devas’ distress and appeal to Shiva. [Chapter 10](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226151.html), 1–9 and 24–44, supports bow, Ganesha worship, alignment, blazing arrow, destruction and the distinction between former devotion and later abandonment. This is a brief introduction, not a complete retelling of the intervening theological episodes. The cities are not described as uniformly evil from their creation; their initial prosperity and Shiva worship remain in the narrative. No invented dialogue or private motives are added. Puranic narrative is presented as sacred tradition, not verified modern history.
- [IIT Kanpur, Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.3.28](https://heritage.iitk.ac.in/BRU/1.3.28) verifies the Sanskrit line printed on poster 6. The gallery explicitly explains that “तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय” is a general prayer for light, not a festival-specific mantra. No added Sanskrit quotation decorates the main article.
