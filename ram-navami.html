Who is Lord Rama?

Maryada Purushottam — The Perfect Man — 7th Avatar of Lord Vishnu

Lord Rama is the embodiment of Dharma — righteousness in human form.

In Hindu theology, Lord Rama is the seventh avatar of Lord Vishnu, born in the Treta Yuga to defeat demon king Ravana and restore righteousness. He is called Maryada Purushottam — the man who upholds all boundaries of virtue — because throughout his life, no matter how great the hardship, he never abandoned his principles. His life is not the story of a god who was always comfortable. It is the story of a man who chose to be righteous even when it cost him everything.

👑

The Ideal Son

Rama gave up the throne of Ayodhya without a word of complaint, solely to honour his father's promise to his stepmother. No argument. No resentment. Just love, obedience and grace — the perfect demonstration of filial duty that inspired generations.
💑

The Ideal Husband

Rama crossed an ocean and fought the most powerful demon king on earth to bring Sita home. His devotion to one partner is held as the highest standard of marital fidelity in Hindu tradition. Their love is the most celebrated union in Indian culture.
🤝

The Ideal Brother

Lakshmana left his own wife and comforts to accompany Rama. Bharata refused the throne and placed Rama's sandals on it — ruling only as regent. Such was the depth of brotherhood in Rama's family. Rama's love for his brothers was equally complete.
🫂

The Ideal Friend

Rama's friendship with Sugreeva the exiled monkey king, Vibhishana the demon's righteous brother, and above all Hanuman — shows that for Rama, loyalty and virtue mattered far more than birth or social status. He treated all with complete dignity.
🏛️

The Ideal King — Ram Rajya

Ram Rajya — Rama's reign — is still India's gold standard of governance: a kingdom where every person is healthy, educated, just and free from fear. Even today leaders invoke Ram Rajya as the ultimate aspiration. No one went hungry. No door needed a lock.
🏹

The Ideal Warrior

Rama broke Shiva's bow at the Swayamvara. His archery was unparalleled. He defeated every demon army placed before him. Yet he never fought from ego — only from duty. He wept for enemies he was forced to kill. This is the dharmic warrior's code.

The Birth of Lord Rama

Valmiki Ramayana · Balakanda · Chapter 18

ततो यज्ञ समाप्ते तु ऋतूनां षट्सु वत्ययः
तत: षण्मास्यतीते च चैत्रे नावमिके तिथौ
Tato Yajna Sampate Tu Ritunam Shatsu Vatyayah
Tatah Shanmasya Tite Cha Chaitre Navamike Tithau
"After the Yajna was completed, after six seasons had passed — on the Navami of Chaitra — Rama was born."
— Valmiki Ramayana, Balakanda, Chapter 18
1

The King's Sorrow

King Dasharatha of Ayodhya was a great and just ruler. Under his reign the kingdom flourished in peace and prosperity. But despite having three queens — Kaushalya, Kaikeyi and Sumitra — he had no children. For years he prayed, performed yagnas, and sought the guidance of sages. The absence of an heir was the one shadow over his golden kingdom.
2

The Putrakameshti Yagna

On the advice of sage Vashistha, Dasharatha organised the Putrakameshti Yagna — a sacred fire ritual performed by the great sage Rishyasringa. As the rituals reached their culmination, a divine being emerged from the sacred fire carrying a golden pot filled with payasam — sacred rice pudding. The celestial messenger declared: "The Lord of the Universe grants you this divine gift."
3

The Sacred Payasam

Dasharatha distributed the payasam among his three queens. Kaushalya received half, Kaikeyi a quarter, and Sumitra the remaining quarter. In due time: Kaushalya gave birth to Rama, Kaikeyi gave birth to Bharata, and Sumitra gave birth to twins — Lakshmana and Shatrughna. The kingdom celebrated for days. The four princes were the answer to every prayer.
4

The Cosmic Moment of Birth

Lord Rama was born on the ninth day (Navami) of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, under the Punarvasu Nakshatra, at the exact midpoint of the day — Madhyahna — when the sun was at its zenith. The scriptures describe the cosmos itself celebrating: gods scattered flowers from the heavens, celestial musicians played, the entire creation rejoiced. In 2026, this sacred moment falls at 12:27 PM on Thursday, 26 March.

The Ayodhya Ram Mandir

Consecrated 22 January 2024 · Sacred seat of Ram Lalla

After 500 years — Ram Lalla has returned to his home.

The Ram Mandir in Ayodhya was consecrated on 22 January 2024 at the site Hindus believe to be Lord Rama's birthplace — Ram Janmabhoomi. The Supreme Court of India awarded the site to Hindus in November 2019. PM Modi performed the Prana Pratishtha ceremony. Full construction was completed 25 November 2025 with the Dharma Dhwaja hoisting.

🌞 The Surya Tilak Miracle
On every Ram Navami at noon, sunlight is directed through a precisely calibrated system of mirrors and lenses to anoint the forehead of Ram Lalla's idol — the Surya Tilak. Uses brass, not iron or steel. No electricity required. Pure astronomical geometry. The first Surya Tilak was performed on 17 April 2024 — the first Ram Navami after consecration — watched live by millions with profound emotion.

How India Celebrates Ram Navami

From Ayodhya to the global diaspora · 26 March 2026

🏛️

Ayodhya — The Epicentre

Ram Navami at Ayodhya draws millions. The Sarayu river bank is lit with diyas at dawn. The Ram Mandir receives lakhs of devotees queuing through the night. At 12:27 PM, the Surya Tilak anoints Ram Lalla's forehead — visible live worldwide. Grand Rath Yatras, Bhandara community feasts, the city luminous with devotion and joy.
🎵

South India — Sri Rama Kalyanam

In Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, Ram Navami is celebrated as the celestial wedding ceremony of Rama and Sita. Most famous at Bhadrachalam temple in Telangana where the state government provides the wedding ornaments. Thyagaraja Kritis music concerts and Harikatha sessions across temples.
📖

North India — Ramayana Recitation

Week-long recitations of the Ramcharitmanas in every neighbourhood. 24-hour Akhand Paath in temples. Ram Lalla Jhula (cradle rocking ceremony for infant Rama) at noon. Community bhandara feeding thousands. The sacred text fills the air across every town and village.
🌍

Global Diaspora

ISKCON temples worldwide observe with full-day fasting, abhishekam, kirtans and elaborate feasts after noon. Hindu diaspora in the UK, Netherlands, USA, Canada, South Africa, Fiji, Mauritius and beyond gathers at temples. Ram Navami is a truly global celebration of dharma.

Ram Navami Puja Vidhi

How to observe with devotion — at home and in temples

🌅
Brahma Muhurat — Start the Day

Wake before sunrise at 4:48 AM. Bathe and wear clean clothes — preferably yellow or saffron, the colours sacred to Lord Vishnu. Offer water to the Sun God with a copper vessel, chanting: ॐ श्री रामाय नमः

🪷
Setting Up the Puja

Place a yellow cloth on the puja platform. Install idols or pictures of Lord Rama, Goddess Sita, Lakshmana and Lord Hanuman together — the complete Ram Darbar. Place a Tulsi plant (sacred to Vishnu). Light a diya. Set up the Kalash (sacred water pot with mango leaves and coconut).

🌿
Fasting — Navami Vrat

Sattvic fast throughout the day: fruits, milk, sabudana khichdi, kuttu (buckwheat) roti, singhara flour, sendha salt. The fast is broken after the noon puja and aarti with prasad distribution. Even a partial fast from sunrise to noon is meritorious.

☀️
Noon Puja — Madhyahna 11:13 AM–1:41 PM

Perform the main puja during the auspicious Madhyahna window. Offer yellow flowers, sandalwood paste, fruits, sweets and Tulsi leaves. Rock the cradle (Jhula) symbolising the infant Rama. Ring the bell 7 times — for Rama, the 7th avatar. Perform aarti at exactly 12:27 PM.

📿
Ramayana Recitation

Read or listen to the Ramayana or Ramcharitmanas. Even one chapter is highly meritorious. The Sundarkand — Hanuman's journey to Lanka — is especially auspicious today. Chant Rama Naam 108 times or sing continuously: Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram.

🍚
Prasad & Breaking Fast

Prasad for Ram Navami: Panjiri (wheat flour with dry fruits and sugar), Kheer (rice pudding), Panchaamrit and fresh fruits. Share prasad with all family members and neighbours. Community bhandara meals are organised at temples.

Sacred Shlokas & The Name of Rama

The Taraka Mantra — two syllables that carry you across existence

RA + MA = the two most powerful syllables in Sanskrit.

"RA" is from the Ashtakshara mantra of Vishnu — the fire syllable that burns all karma. "MA" is from the Panchakshara mantra of Shiva — the seed of liberation. Together: RAMA = the essence of both Vishnu and Shiva in two sounds. The sages called it the Taraka Mantra — the mantra that carries you across the ocean of existence.

The Core Mantra
ॐ श्री रामाय नमः
Om Shri Ramaya Namah
Salutations to Lord Rama. Chant 108 times at noon on Ram Navami. This is the most complete and powerful Ram mantra — seven syllables that encapsulate the complete divine.
From Valmiki Ramayana — Aranya Kanda
रामो विग्रहवान् धर्मः
साधुः सत्य-पराक्रमः
Ramo Vigrahavan Dharmah Sadhuh Satya-Parakramah
"Rama is Dharma in human form — virtuous, with courage rooted in truth." The most direct and beautiful definition of who Rama is, from Valmiki himself.
The Taraka Mantra — Chant Continuously
श्री राम जय राम जय जय राम
Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram
The taraka mantra of the Ramayana tradition. Tulsidas called this the essence of all prayer. It can be chanted at any pace, at any time, anywhere. Many saints completed millions of repetitions of this mantra.
Shiva Tells Parvati the Secret
राम रामेति रामेति रमे रामे मनोरमे
Rama Rameti Rameti Rame Rame Manorame
"Chanting Rama, Rama, Rama — I delight in Rama, Rama, who enchants the mind." Lord Shiva tells Parvati that three repetitions of the name Rama equals the entire Vishnu Sahasranama (1,000 names of Vishnu).
Tulsidas — Ramcharitmanas
मंगल भवन अमंगल हारी
द्रवहु सुदसरथ अजर बिहारी
Mangal Bhavan Amangal Hari · Dravahu Sudasarath Ajar Bihari
"Auspicious abode, destroyer of all inauspiciousness — flow into me, O eternal son of Dasharatha." One of the most beloved couplets from the Ramcharitmanas, sung at virtually every Ram temple in North India.

Rama for the Modern World

Timeless lessons — as relevant in 2026 as in the Treta Yuga

Rama did not have an easy life. He was the most qualified person in his world — and still went to the forest.

What he showed: your circumstances do not determine your character. Your choices do. The Ramayana is not mythology stored in the past — it is a guide to every challenge a human being faces today.

For Everyone — This Ram Navami
  • Light one diya at noon: At 12:27 PM, the exact moment of Rama's birth, light one lamp. Take 60 seconds of silence. That is your celebration.
  • Choose one quality to embody today: Rama had seven aspects of perfection — son, husband, brother, friend, king, warrior, devotee. Choose one for the day.
  • Share one Ramayana story: Tell a family member about Shabari's berries, or Hanuman lifting the mountain. Keeping these stories alive is seva.
  • Fast one meal: Skip lunch. Notice the clarity that comes. Fast is not punishment — it is attention paid to what nourishes you beyond food.
For the Diaspora — Celebrating Away from India
  • Video call family at 12:27 PM IST: Ayodhya is 3.5 hours ahead of Rotterdam, 4.5 ahead of London. Calculate your local time and celebrate together.
  • Make kheer or panjiri: Cooking prasad is itself devotion. The smell of cardamom and saffron in your kitchen is the festival, wherever you are.
  • Share this guide: Forward to family and friends. The Ramayana belongs to everyone — Hindu or not. It is a story about what it means to be human.
  • Find your local temple: Even in the Netherlands there are Hindu temples in Rotterdam, Amsterdam and The Hague that observe Ram Navami.
For Young Adults & Students
  • Map Rama's qualities to your life: Brahmacharini (from Navratri) and Rama both teach the same thing: your formative years require sustained, repeated effort. What discipline are you building right now?
  • Read the Sundarkand: Hanuman's leap of faith is the chapter for young people. When you cannot identify the solution, sometimes you carry the whole mountain. Read it today.
  • For music producers: Rama's story has inspired music for 3,000 years. From Thyagaraja Kritis to modern devotional. Today is a good day to create something with intention.
