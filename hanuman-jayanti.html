Who is Lord Hanuman?

The most widely worshipped deity in India today

Hanuman is the meeting point of two worlds — infinite divine power and complete selfless surrender.

He is simultaneously the strongest being in creation and its most humble servant. Son of the wind god Vayu, avatar of Lord Shiva, greatest devotee of Lord Rama — Hanuman bridges every tradition in Hinduism. He is worshipped by Shaivas (as Shiva's avatar), Vaishnavas (as Rama's devotee), wrestlers and soldiers (for strength), students (for focus and wisdom), and families (for protection). More temples are dedicated to Hanuman in India than any other deity. His name is on more lips every day than almost any other — from the Hanuman Chalisa chanted by 100 million people daily to the simple greeting "Jai Bajrang Bali" said at shrines across every state.

The Birth of Lord Hanuman

Where Shiva's will, Vayu's grace and Anjana's devotion converged

1

Anjana's Curse and Penance

Anjana was Punjikasthala — a celestial apsara of extraordinary beauty in Indra's court. One day she laughed at a sage deep in meditation. The sage cursed her to be reborn as a monkey on earth. She could only be freed when she gave birth to an incarnation of Lord Shiva. Anjana accepted her fate with grace. Descending to earth, she married the brave vanara king Kesari, ruler of Sumeru. For years the couple prayed intensely to Lord Shiva and to Vayu, the wind god, for a divine child.
2

The Divine Plan

In the heavens, the Trinity was planning the defeat of Ravana. Vishnu would incarnate as Rama. Shiva was asked to incarnate as a great devotee who would serve Rama and make his victory possible. Shiva agreed. Simultaneously in Ayodhya, King Dasharatha performed the Putrakameshti Yajna — the same fire ritual that gave sacred payasam (pudding) to his three queens, leading to the births of Rama, Lakshmana, Bharata and Shatrughna. By divine will, a kite snatched a fragment of this sacred payasam. Vayu guided that fragment across the sky — all the way to the outstretched hands of Anjana, deep in prayer in the forest of Kishkindha.
3

The Birth — Chaitra Purnima at Sunrise

Anjana consumed the sacred pudding. Vayu spoke to her: "From this moment you carry a son endowed with the strength of mountains, the speed of the wind, the wisdom of Brihaspati and the courage of the gods. He will be among the greatest beings in all creation." On the full moon of Chaitra, as the sun rose over the Anjanadri hill in Kishkindha, Anjana gave birth to a radiant monkey-faced child. At the moment of birth the curse was lifted — Anjana was free. She had brought Lord Shiva himself into the world as her son. The child was named Anjaneya (son of Anjana), and later became known by his most famous name: Hanuman.
4

The Child Who Tried to Eat the Sun

The infant Hanuman was extraordinary from his first day. One morning, hungry and full of curiosity, he looked up and saw the rising sun — glowing red and round. He thought it was a ripe mango. He leaped into the sky with the speed of the wind to catch it. At that same moment, the demon Rahu was approaching the sun to cause an eclipse. Hanuman thrashed Rahu away and flew straight toward the sun. Indra, watching in alarm, threw his thunderbolt (Vajra) at the child. The blow struck Hanuman's jaw — giving him the name Hanuman, "one with a broken jaw" — and he fell unconscious to earth. Vayu was devastated. In his grief he withdrew all air from the three worlds. Creation began to suffocate. The gods rushed to Vayu. Brahma revived Hanuman. The gods then blessed the child with extraordinary gifts: Agni blessed him with immunity from fire; Varuna from water; Indra made him stronger than the Vajra itself; Brahma gave him the ability to travel anywhere; Shiva declared him immortal — a Chiranjivi, living as long as Rama's name is spoken on earth.

The Powers of Lord Hanuman

Blessed by every god at birth · Limitless by devotion

💪

Ashta Siddhis — Eight Supernatural Powers

Hanuman possesses all eight great siddhis: Anima (becoming microscopic), Mahima (becoming vast as a mountain), Garima (becoming infinitely heavy), Laghima (becoming weightless and flying), Prapti (reaching anywhere instantly), Prakamya (irresistible willpower), Ishitva (mastery over all creation), Vashitva (control over all forces). He also holds Nava Nidhis — the nine divine treasures. These are not merely supernatural — they represent complete mastery over the material world through spiritual discipline.
🌊

Immunity Granted by the Gods

After the Vajra incident, every major god blessed the infant. Fire cannot burn him (Agni's boon). Water cannot drown him (Varuna's boon). He is faster than the wind itself (Vayu's boon). No weapon can kill him (Brahma's boon). He can change his form at will. He is a Chiranjivi — immortal for as long as Rama's name is chanted on earth. Even death cannot claim him by force — Hanuman declared he will die only by his own will, when the time is right.
📚

Extraordinary Wisdom and Learning

Hanuman is described as the greatest scholar in the world. He learned all four Vedas and nine grammars directly from the Sun God Surya — while flying backwards in front of him so as not to turn his back on the teacher. He mastered Sanskrit grammar perfectly. When he first spoke to Rama, Rama told Lakshmana: "Whoever sent this messenger has profound wisdom — no enemy could speak this beautifully." Tulsidas calls him: Buddhi-matam Varishtham — the greatest of the wise.
💗

The Power of Bhakti

Of all Hanuman's powers, the greatest is his bhakti — devotion so pure it exceeds all siddhis. When Sita asked what reward he wanted for finding her, Hanuman said: "Only to serve Rama." When Rama offered him any boon, Hanuman said: "Only to live as long as your name is chanted." When someone once asked him what Rama means to him, Hanuman tore open his chest — and inside, carved into his heart, were the images of Rama and Sita. This is why the Ramcharitmanas says: Bharat Milap bina Rama Ko, nahi pyara Hanuman. Without Hanuman, there is no Rama.

Hanuman in the Ramayana

Every great moment of the Ramayana passes through Hanuman

Without Hanuman, Rama could not have won. Valmiki himself says so.

Hanuman appears first in the Kishkindha Kanda when he approaches Rama and Lakshmana in disguise as a brahmin — to test them on behalf of the suspicious Sugreeva. From that first encounter, his fate is woven with Rama's. He does not just help Rama — he makes the entire Ramayana possible.

1

The First Meeting — Rama Recognises Greatness

When Hanuman first spoke to Rama, disguised as a brahmin, Rama was astonished by his language. He told Lakshmana: "This person has mastered all four Vedas — his speech is perfect, his grammar flawless, his voice moves the soul. No enemy could have sent someone like this." Before they even knew who Hanuman was, Rama had already seen his greatness. When Hanuman revealed himself, prostrating at Rama's feet, Rama lifted him up and embraced him — two avatars meeting for the first time. All of creation held its breath.
2

The Leap to Lanka — 500 Yojanas Alone

All the search parties sent by Sugreeva failed — except one. Only Hanuman's party reached the southern shore where Lanka lay. Standing at the edge of the ocean — 500 yojanas (roughly 6,500 km in cosmic measure) across — the other vanaras despaired. Jambavan, the ancient bear, reminded Hanuman of his powers. Hanuman had been cursed as a child to forget his own strength until reminded. The moment Jambavan spoke, Hanuman remembered. He grew to the size of a mountain, filled his lungs, and leaped across the ocean in a single bound. The sea parted beneath him. Lanka trembled at his arrival. He found Sita in the Ashoka garden, delivered Rama's ring, consoled her, burned Lanka with his flaming tail after being deliberately captured, and returned with the intelligence that saved the entire mission.
3

The Mountain Moment — Dronagiri

During the Lanka War, Indrajit's Brahmastra paralysed the entire Vanara army — including Rama and Lakshmana. Only Jambavan survived. He told the barely conscious Hanuman about the Sanjeevani herb on Mount Dronagiri in the Himalayas. Hanuman flew to the Himalayas instantly. On the mountain he found dozens of glowing herbs — but could not identify which was the Sanjeevani. He had a choice: return empty-handed and fail, or do something extraordinary. Hanuman chose the extraordinary. He tore the entire mountain from its roots, lifted it above his head, and flew back to Lanka carrying Dronagiri itself. The army was revived. The war continued. Victory was possible. This single act is Hanuman at his most essential: when you cannot identify the solution, you carry the whole mountain.
4

The Ring and the Jewel

Before Hanuman leaped to Lanka, Rama pressed his ring into Hanuman's hand — a token by which Sita would know the messenger was genuine. When Hanuman found Sita and showed her the ring, she wept with relief and joy. She gave Hanuman her Chudamani — a jewelled ornament from her hair — to carry back to Rama as proof he had found her. Two objects carried across an ocean in both directions — Rama's faith going one way, Sita's hope returning the other — with Hanuman as the bridge between them. When Hanuman placed the Chudamani in Rama's hands, Rama wept and embraced Hanuman: "What can I give you? My whole kingdom cannot repay this." Then the Avatar of Vishnu embraced the Avatar of Shiva — and the universe was complete.

The Hanuman Chalisa

By Goswami Tulsidas · 1574 CE · Awadhi · The world's most recited prayer

Understanding the Chalisa

The Hanuman Chalisa (Chalisa = forty) was composed by saint-poet Tulsidas in 1574 CE in Varanasi, in the Awadhi language. It is structured as: 2 opening Dohas (couplets) + 40 Chaupais (four-line verses) + 1 closing Doha = 43 verses total. An estimated 100 million people recite it every day. It describes Hanuman's form, his qualities, his deeds in the Ramayana, and the blessings he grants. Tulsidas wrote it after receiving Hanuman's darshan at the Sankat Mochan temple in Varanasi, which he himself founded.

Opening Dohas — Invocation

दोहा 1 Doha · Opening · Guru Vandana
श्रीगुरु चरन सरोज रज, निज मनु मुकुरु सुधारि।
बरनउँ रघुबर बिमल जसु, जो दायकु फल चारि॥
Shri Guru Charan Saroj Raj, Nij Manu Mukuru Sudhaari
Baranau Raghuvar Bimal Jasu, Jo Daayaku Phal Chaari
Having purified the mirror of my mind with the dust of my Guru's lotus feet, I describe the pure glory of Sri Rama (the best of Raghu's dynasty), which bestows the four fruits of life.
Tulsidas begins not with Hanuman but with his Guru — the highest tradition in Indian spirituality. The "four fruits" are Dharma (righteousness), Artha (prosperity), Kama (love) and Moksha (liberation).
दोहा 2 Doha · Opening · Prayer for Help
बुद्धिहीन तनु जानिके, सुमिरौं पवन-कुमार।
बल बुद्धि विद्या देहु मोहिं, हरहु कलेस विकार॥
Buddhi-heen Tanu Jaanike, Sumiraun Pavan-Kumaar
Bal Buddhi Vidya Dehu Mohi, Harahu Kales Vikaar
Knowing this body to be devoid of intelligence, I remember the Son of Wind. Grant me strength, wisdom and knowledge — and remove all my afflictions and impurities.
Tulsidas calls himself foolish and asks Hanuman for three specific gifts: Bal (strength), Buddhi (wisdom) and Vidya (knowledge). This is why students recite the Chalisa before exams.

Chaupais 1–10 — Hanuman's Form and Qualities

1 Chaupai · Hanuman's Appearance
जय हनुमान ज्ञान गुन सागर।
जय कपीस तिहुँ लोक उजागर॥
Jai Hanumaan Gyaan Gun Saagar
Jai Kapees Tihu Lok Ujaagar
Glory to Hanuman — ocean of wisdom and virtue. Glory to the Lord of Monkeys who illuminates all three worlds.
2 Chaupai · Hanuman's Form
रामदूत अतुलित बलधामा।
अञ्जनि-पुत्र पवनसुत नामा॥
Raamdoot Atulit Bal Dhaama
Anjani-Putra Pavan-Sut Naama
Messenger of Rama, abode of incomparable strength. Known by the names Son of Anjana and Son of the Wind.
3 Chaupai · His Golden Form
महावीर विक्रम बजरंगी।
कुमति निवार सुमति के संगी॥
Mahaveer Vikram Bajrangi
Kumati Nivaar Sumati Ke Sangi
Great hero, valiant warrior, one whose limbs are hard as diamond. Destroyer of evil thoughts, companion of righteous minds.
Bajrangi — diamond-limbed — connects to the Vajra (Indra's thunderbolt) that struck his jaw. What should have destroyed him instead made him invincible.
4 Chaupai · His Appearance
कञ्चन बरन बिराज सुबेसा।
कानन कुंडल कुञ्चित केसा॥
Kanchan Baran Biraaj Subesa
Kaanan Kundal Kunchit Kesa
Golden in complexion, resplendent in beautiful attire. Wearing ear-rings, with curly hair flowing.
5 Chaupai · His Weapons
हाथ बज्र औ ध्वजा बिराजै।
काँधे मूँज जनेऊ साजै॥
Haath Bajra Au Dhwaja Birajai
Kaandhe Moonj Janeu Sajai
In his hands the thunderbolt (Vajra) and a banner shine magnificently. On his shoulder a sacred thread of Munja grass is worn.
6 Chaupai · Shankara's Incarnation
शंकर सुवन केसरी नंदन।
तेज प्रताप महाजग वंदन॥
Shankar Suvan Kesari Nandan
Tej Pratap Maha Jag Vandan
Son of Shankara (Shiva), son of Kesari — radiant with glory, worshipped across the entire great world.
This verse is crucial — Tulsidas explicitly calls Hanuman the son of Shiva. In Shaiva tradition Hanuman is Shiva's 11th Rudra avatar, sent to serve Vishnu's Rama incarnation.
7 Chaupai · Wisdom and Devotion
विद्यावान गुनी अति चातुर।
राम काज करिबे को आतुर॥
Vidyavaan Guni Ati Chaatur
Ram Kaaj Karibe Ko Aatur
Master of all knowledge, virtuous, supremely clever. Always eager to serve the work of Rama.
The combination described here — greatest scholar + completely selfless servant — is the paradox at Hanuman's heart. Ego dissolves entirely in service.
8 Chaupai · Ram's Mission
प्रभु चरित्र सुनिबे को रसिया।
राम लखन सीता मन बसिया॥
Prabhu Charitra Sunibe Ko Rasiya
Ram Lakhan Sita Man Basiya
He delights in hearing the glories of the Lord. Ram, Lakshmana and Sita live permanently in his mind and heart.
9 Chaupai · Sukshma Form
सूक्ष्म रूप धरि सियहिं दिखावा।
बिकट रूप धरि लंक जरावा॥
Sookshma Roop Dhari Siyahi Dikhaawa
Bikat Roop Dhari Lanka Jaraawa
He took a tiny form to appear before Sita. Then took a terrifying form to set Lanka ablaze.
The genius of Hanuman: in Sita's presence he made himself small so as not to frighten her. Then he expanded to fill Lanka with fire. Same being — different purposes, perfect judgment.
10 Chaupai · The Mountain
भीम रूप धरि असुर सँहारे।
रामचन्द्र के काज सँवारे॥
Bheem Roop Dhari Asur Sanhaare
Ramchandra Ke Kaaj Sanvaare
Taking a fearsome Bhima-like form he destroyed the demons. And thus accomplished the work of Ramachandra.

Chaupais 11–20 — Hanuman's Deeds in the Ramayana

11 Chaupai · Reviving Lakshmana
लाय सञ्जीवन लखन जियाये।
श्री रघुबीर हरषि उर लाये॥
Laay Sanjeevan Lakhan Jiyaaye
Shri Raghubeer Harashi Ur Laaye
He brought the Sanjeevani herb and revived Lakshmana. Sri Raghubeer (Rama) embraced him joyfully to his heart.
When Rama embraced Hanuman after Lakshmana's revival, he said: "You are as dear to me as Bharata." This is the highest comparison Rama could make — Bharata, his beloved brother who refused the throne.
12 Chaupai · Praised by Rama
रघुपति कीन्ही बहुत बड़ाई।
तुम मम प्रिय भरतहि सम भाई॥
Raghupati Keehi Bahut Badaai
Tum Mam Priya Bharatahi Sam Bhai
Raghupati (Rama) praised him greatly: "You are as dear to me as my beloved brother Bharata."
13 Chaupai · Praised by All
सहस बदन तुम्हरो जस गावैं।
अस कहि श्रीपति कण्ठ लगावैं॥
Sahas Badan Tumharo Jas Gaavain
As Kahi Shreepati Kanth Lagaavain
A thousand mouths sing your glory — saying this, Shreepati (Rama) embraced Hanuman to his throat (heart).
14 Chaupai · Sanak and the Sages
सनकादिक ब्रह्मादि मुनीसा।
नारद सारद सहित अहीसा॥
Sankaadik Brahmaadi Muneesa
Naarad Saarad Sahit Aheesa
Sanaka and other sages, Brahma and the sages, Narada, Sharada (Saraswati) along with Shesha — all sing your praise.
15 Chaupai · Yam and Kubera
जम कुबेर दिगपाल जहाँ ते।
कबि कोबिद कहि सकैं कहाँ ते॥
Jam Kuber Digpaal Jahaan Te
Kabi Kobid Kahi Sakain Kahaan Te
Yama, Kubera and the guardians of the eight directions — even poets and scholars cannot fully describe your glory.
16 Chaupai · Sugreeva's Kingdom
तुम उपकार सुग्रीवहिं कीन्हा।
राम मिलाय राज पद दीन्हा॥
Tum Upkaar Sugreevahi Keenha
Raam Milaay Raaj Pad Deenha
You did great service for Sugreeva — by introducing him to Rama and helping him regain his kingdom.
17 Chaupai · Vibhishana
तुम्हरो मन्त्र विभीषण माना।
लंकेस्वर भए सब जग जाना॥
Tumharo Mantr Vibheeshan Maana
Lankeshwar Bhaye Sab Jag Jaana
Vibhishana accepted your counsel — and became the lord of Lanka, as the whole world knows.
It was Hanuman who advised Vibhishana to seek Rama's refuge. Without Hanuman's guidance, Ravana's righteous brother might have remained in Lanka and the war's outcome might have differed.
18 Chaupai · Distance of the Sun
जुग सहस्र जोजन पर भानू।
लील्यो ताहि मधुर फल जानू॥
Jug Sahasr Yojan Par Bhaanu
Leelyo Taahi Madhur Phal Jaanu
The sun is thousands of yugas and yojanas away — yet you swallowed it thinking it a sweet fruit in your childhood.
Scholars have calculated "Jug Sahasr Yojan" (12,000 × 1000 yojana) and found it remarkably close to the actual Earth-Sun distance. Whether literal or symbolic, it speaks of Hanuman's boundless reach.
19 Chaupai · The Ring
प्रभु मुद्रिका मेलि मुख माहीं।
जलधि लाँघि गये अचरज नाहीं॥
Prabhu Mudrika Meli Mukh Maahin
Jaladhi Laanghi Gaye Acharaj Naahin
Holding the Lord's ring in your mouth, you crossed the ocean — there is no surprise in this for one like you.
20 Chaupai · Beyond Description
दुर्गम काज जगत के जेते।
सुगम अनुग्रह तुम्हरे तेते॥
Durgam Kaaj Jagat Ke Jete
Sugam Anugraha Tumhare Tete
All the difficult tasks of the world — they all become easy by your grace.
This is the most practical verse of the Chalisa. The promise is direct: whatever seems impossible becomes achievable through Hanuman's blessing. Not through magic — through the courage and clarity that devotion brings.

Chaupais 21–30 — Protection, Healing and Grace

21 Chaupai · Hanuman's Grace
राम दुआरे तुम रखवारे।
होत न आज्ञा बिनु पैसारे॥
Raam Duaare Tum Rakhwaare
Hot Na Aagya Binu Paisaare
You are the guardian at Rama's gate. No one enters without your permission.
This verse explains why Hanuman is worshipped first among Rama's devotees. To reach Rama, you must first earn Hanuman's grace. He is both the gate and the gatekeeper of Rama's realm.
22 Chaupai · The Power of His Name
सब सुख लहैं तुम्हारी सरना।
तुम रक्षक काहू को डरना॥
Sab Sukh Lahai Tumhari Sarna
Tum Rakshak Kaahu Ko Darna
All joys are obtained in your shelter. With you as protector, there is nothing to fear.
23 Chaupai · Protection from All Harm
आपन तेज सम्हारो आपै।
तीनों लोक हाँक तें काँपै॥
Aapan Tej Samhaaro Aapai
Teenon Lok Haank Te Kaampai
You alone can contain your own radiance. At your single shout, all three worlds tremble.
24 Chaupai · Protection from Evil Forces
भूत पिसाच निकट नहिं आवै।
महाबीर जब नाम सुनावै॥
Bhoot Pisaach Nikat Nahin Aavai
Mahaveer Jab Naam Sunaavai
No ghost or evil spirit can come near — when Mahaveer (Hanuman's) name is spoken.
This verse is why Hanuman's name is chanted in moments of fear or vulnerability. The power is not superstition — it is the courage and clarity that comes from connecting with the fearless.
25 Chaupai · Disease and Suffering
नासै रोग हरै सब पीरा।
जपत निरन्तर हनुमत बीरा॥
Naasai Rog Harai Sab Peera
Japat Nirantar Hanumat Beera
Disease is destroyed and all suffering removed — by continuously chanting the name of brave Hanuman.
26 Chaupai · Freeing from Troubles
संकट तें हनुमान छुड़ावै।
मन क्रम बचन ध्यान जो लावै॥
Sankat Ten Hanumaan Chudaavai
Man Kram Bachan Dhyan Jo Laavai
Hanuman frees one from all troubles — for one who keeps him in thought, deed and word.
The condition here is important: thought + deed + word — all three aligned. Hanuman doesn't protect those who merely chant while acting wrongly. The protection requires integrity.
27 Chaupai · Rama's Devotee
सब पर राम तपस्वी राजा।
तिन के काज सकल तुम साजा॥
Sab Par Raam Tapasvi Raaja
Tin Ke Kaaj Sakal Tum Saaja
Rama is the ascetic king above all. You accomplish all his works.
28 Chaupai · Whoever Seeks Hanuman
और मनोरथ जो कोई लावै।
सोई अमित जीवन फल पावै॥
Aur Manorath Jo Koi Laavai
Soi Amit Jeevan Phal Paavai
And whoever brings any other wish to you — they receive unlimited fruits in their life.
29 Chaupai · Ruling All Four Ages
चारों जुग परताप तुम्हारा।
है परसिद्ध जगत उजियारा॥
Chaaron Jug Partap Tumhaara
Hai Parsiddh Jagat Ujiyaara
Your glory shines across all four ages (Yugas). You are famous — the light of the world.
30 Chaupai · Eternal Presence
साधु सन्त के तुम रखवारे।
असुर निकंदन राम दुलारे॥
Saadhu Sant Ke Tum Rakhwaare
Asur Nikandan Raam Dulaare
You are the protector of saints and sages, destroyer of demons, Rama's most beloved.

Chaupais 31–40 — The Devotee's Prayer and Tulsidas's Surrender

31 Chaupai · Ashta Siddhi Nava Nidhi
अष्ट सिद्धि नव निधि के दाता।
अस बर दीन्ह जानकी माता॥
Asht Siddhi Nav Nidhi Ke Daata
As Bar Deenh Jaanki Maata
You are the bestower of the eight siddhis (supernatural powers) and nine treasures (nidhis). Such a boon was given by Mother Janaki (Sita) herself.
Sita herself blessed Hanuman with the power to grant all siddhis. This is why Hanuman is considered the only deity who can grant these powers — they come from Sita's blessing, backed by Rama's grace.
32 Chaupai · Serving Rama Always
राम रसायन तुम्हरे पासा।
सदा रहो रघुपति के दासा॥
Raam Rasaayan Tumhare Paasa
Sadaa Raho Raghupati Ke Daasa
You possess the elixir of Rama — always remain the servant of Raghupati (Rama).
33 Chaupai · Wherever Rama is Sung
तुम्हरे भजन राम को पावै।
जनम जनम के दुख बिसरावै॥
Tumhare Bhajan Raam Ko Paavai
Janam Janam Ke Dukh Bisaraavai
Through your devotional worship one reaches Rama and forgets the sorrows of countless births.
34 Chaupai · After This Life
अन्त काल रघुबर पुर जाई।
जहाँ जन्म हरिभक्त कहाई॥
Ant Kaal Raghubar Pur Jaai
Jahaan Janm Haribhakt Kahaayi
At the end of life one goes to Rama's abode — and wherever one is reborn, one is known as Hari's devotee.
35 Chaupai · Other Deities
और देवता चित्त न धरई।
हनुमत सेई सर्ब सुख करई॥
Aur Devata Chitt Na Dharai
Hanumat Sei Sarb Sukh Karai
One need not hold any other deity in mind — serving Hanuman bestows all happiness.
A bold verse — not dismissing other deities, but saying Hanuman is complete: through him one reaches Shiva (his origin), Vishnu/Rama (his master) and all the gods who blessed him. Worship Hanuman and you worship all.
36 Chaupai · Troubles Removed
संकट कटै मिटै सब पीरा।
जो सुमिरै हनुमत बलबीरा॥
Sankat Katai Mitai Sab Peera
Jo Sumirai Hanumat Balbeera
All troubles are cut away, all suffering removed — for one who remembers brave and mighty Hanuman.
37 Chaupai · Jai Jai Jai
जय जय जय हनुमान गुसाईं।
कृपा करहु गुरु देव की नाईं॥
Jai Jai Jai Hanumaan Gusaain
Kripa Karahu Guru Dev Ki Naain
Victory, victory, victory to Lord Hanuman! Grant me your mercy like a Guru and a god.
38 Chaupai · The Promise of the Chalisa
जो सत बार पाठ कर कोई।
छूटहि बन्दि महा सुख होई॥
Jo Sat Baar Paath Kar Koi
Chhootihi Bandi Maha Sukh Hoi
Whoever recites this Chalisa one hundred times — all bondage is freed and great joy attained.
The "hundred times" is not merely literal — it speaks of sustained, devoted practice. The freedom promised is from the inner bondage of fear, ego and attachment — the real prisons.
39 Chaupai · Shiva as Witness
जो यह पढ़ै हनुमान चालीसा।
होय सिद्धि साखी गौरीसा॥
Jo Yeh Padhai Hanumaan Chalisa
Hoy Siddhi Saakhi Gaurisa
Whoever reads this Hanuman Chalisa — they attain perfection and success. Lord Shiva (Gaurisa — husband of Gauri) himself is the witness.
The cosmic guarantee: Shiva witnesses this promise. And since Hanuman IS Shiva's avatar, Shiva witnesses his own manifestation's promise — the most complete guarantee imaginable.
40 Chaupai · Tulsidas's Surrender
तुलसीदास सदा हरि चेरा।
कीजै नाथ हृदय महँ डेरा॥
Tulsidas Sada Hari Chera
Keejai Nath Hriday Mahan Dera
Tulsidas is always the servant of Hari (God). O Lord, make your home in my heart forever.
The final request: not wealth, not power, not liberation — only one thing: "Live in my heart." This is the essence of bhakti. When the divine lives within you, everything else follows.

Closing Doha — The Final Prayer

दोहा Doha · Closing · The Supreme Request
पवन तनय संकट हरन, मंगल मूरति रूप।
राम लखन सीता सहित, हृदय बसहु सुर भूप॥
Pavan Tanay Sankat Haran, Mangal Moorti Roop
Ram Lakhan Sita Sahit, Hriday Basahu Sur Bhoop
O Son of the Wind, remover of all troubles, embodiment of auspiciousness — together with Rama, Lakshmana and Sita, dwell in my heart, O King of the Gods.
The Chalisa ends where all of Hinduism ends — with the complete Ram Darbar: Rama, Lakshmana and Sita together in the heart. Hanuman brings them all to you. This is why he is called the gateway to Rama.

Tuesday & Saturday — Hanuman's Sacred Days

Why these two days · What to do · The deeper meaning

Tuesday (Mangalvar) is Hanuman's day. Saturday (Shanivar) is equally sacred — for protection from Shani (Saturn).

Hanuman is worshipped especially on these two days across India. Every Tuesday and Saturday evening, millions visit Hanuman temples, light lamps, offer sindoor and oil, and recite the Chalisa. The queues at Hanuman Garhi in Ayodhya or Sankat Mochan in Varanasi on these days stretch for hours. You need no special knowledge — just showing up, lighting a lamp and chanting the name is enough.

📅

Why Tuesday?

Tuesday is Mangalvar — the day of Mangala (Mars). Mars represents energy, courage, war and ambition. Hanuman embodies all these qualities in their highest, most righteous form. Worshipping Hanuman on Tuesday channels Mars energy positively — converting aggression into courage, ambition into service, competition into devotion. It is also believed Hanuman was born on a Tuesday in some traditions.
🪐

Why Saturday? — Shani and Hanuman

Saturday is Shanivar — day of Shani (Saturn), the most feared planet in Jyotish (Hindu astrology). The legend: Shani Dev once tried to place his gaze on Hanuman. Hanuman grabbed Shani in his tail and swung him around until Shani begged for mercy. Hanuman released him on the condition that Shani would never trouble Hanuman's devotees. This is why worshipping Hanuman on Saturday is believed to protect against Saturn's malefic effects, the famous "Shani Dasha" period, and life's most challenging phases.
🪔

What to Do on These Days

Wake early. Bathe. Wear red, saffron or orange — Hanuman's colours. Visit the nearest Hanuman temple before 9 AM if possible. Offer: sindoor (vermillion), sesame oil, marigold flowers, laddoos or besan ke laddoo as prasad. Recite the Hanuman Chalisa once, seven times or 108 times. Fast — fruits and milk — until sunset. In the evening chant Hanuman's 12 names: Hanumaan, Anjaneya, Mahaveer, Bheem Roop, Udadhikraman, Ram Doota, Dasha Greeva Darpa Ha, Lakshmana Pranadaata, Vajra Kaaya, Shikhi Varna, Rameshta, Phalguna Sakha.

Major Hanuman Temples

The most sacred shrines · Where millions seek his darshan

Hanuman Garhi

📍 Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh · 76 steps to the top
One of the most visited temples in India, Hanuman Garhi sits on a hillock in the heart of Ayodhya — the birthplace of Lord Rama. The 10th-century temple contains a Hanuman idol sitting in a commanding posture, surrounded by Anjana Mata above. Hanuman is revered here as the eternal guardian of Ayodhya — sitting watch over the Ram Janmabhoomi. Since the Ram Mandir consecration in January 2024, Ayodhya draws 135 million visitors annually and Hanuman Garhi is the first stop for most pilgrims.
Closes: 10 PM daily Tues/Sat: Massive crowds

Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple

📍 Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh · Founded by Tulsidas himself
The most sacred Hanuman temple in Varanasi — founded by Tulsidas at the exact spot where he received Hanuman's darshan. The name means "Remover of Troubles" — Sankat Mochan. The temple sits on the banks of the Ganga and is open 24 hours. The idol here is unusually warm and life-like according to devotees. The Tuesday evening aarti draws thousands. It was at this temple that the Hanuman Chalisa was first sung publicly by Tulsidas. A terrorist bomb attack in 2006 could not shake devotees' faith — the temple was rebuilt and continues.
Open 24 hours Founded by Tulsidas c.1574

Salasar Balaji

📍 Salasar, Churu District, Rajasthan
One of the most extraordinary Hanuman temples in India. The idol here is unique — Hanuman with a beard and moustache (most Hanuman idols are clean-faced). The idol is swayambhu — self-manifested — discovered in the 18th century by a farmer ploughing his field. The Salasar Balaji is believed to fulfil genuine heartfelt wishes — especially for marriage, children, health and career. Lakhs of devotees visit on Hanuman Jayanti and Purnima nights, many completing the yatra on foot from surrounding states.
Unique bearded idol 18th century swayambhu

Mahakal Hanuman (Ujjain)

📍 Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh · Adjacent to Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga
In Ujjain — the city of Mahakal (Shiva) — there is an ancient Hanuman temple adjacent to the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga. This placement is theologically significant: Shiva and his own avatar Hanuman sitting side by side. The idol here shows Hanuman in the Panchmukhi (five-faced) form — his most powerful manifestation. The five faces represent the five directions and the five elements, making Panchmukhi Hanuman the most complete protective form. Ujjain is one of the four dhams of the Kumbh Mela.
Panchmukhi form Adjacent to Jyotirlinga

Anjanadri Hill — Kishkindha

📍 Near Hampi, Koppal District, Karnataka · Hanuman's birthplace
The most ancient and spiritually resonant Hanuman site — Anjanadri Hill near Hampi (ancient Kishkindha) is the most widely accepted location of Hanuman's birth. A 575-step climb leads to the hilltop temple. At sunrise, standing on this hill looking across the boulder-strewn landscape of Hampi — where Rama and Hanuman first met — is one of the most moving experiences in all of Indian pilgrimage. The Hampi region is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
575 steps · Sunrise recommended Hanuman's birthplace

Hanuman is believed to have been born at sunrise · Temples begin before dawn
🌅
Before Sunrise — Brahma Muhurat

Wake at 4:30 AM. Bathe. Temples across India begin abhishekam — the sacred bathing of Hanuman's idol with Panchamrita (milk, curd, ghee, honey, sugar), then holy water, tulsi and sandalwood. Bells ring continuously. The Hanuman Chalisa is recited without stop from before dawn.

🔴
Sindoor Offering — The Signature Ritual

Hanuman is the only deity offered sindoor (vermillion) as a primary offering — not just on the idol's forehead but applied to his entire body. The legend: when Sita applied sindoor to her parting as a mark of her marriage, Hanuman asked why. She said it was for Rama's long life. Hanuman immediately covered his entire body in sindoor — for even greater protection of Rama. This is why his idols are entirely red-orange.

📿
Hanuman Chalisa — All Day Recitation

Groups of devotees take turns reciting the Chalisa continuously — a relay of devotion that runs from dawn to midnight. Many complete 11, 21, or 108 recitations personally. The Bajrang Baan — a powerful invocation — is also recited at major temples. In Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, a 41-day deeksha (spiritual vow) culminates today.

🏃
Processions — Shobha Yatra

In North India especially, grand Shobha Yatras (processions) carry Hanuman's idol through streets decorated with saffron flags. Devotees in orange robes chant "Jai Bajrang Bali!" Wrestlers and martial artists perform demonstrations — honouring Hanuman as the patron of physical strength and discipline. In some areas, devotees carry weight on their shoulders as an act of devotion — symbolising Hanuman lifting the mountain.

🍛
Bhandara — Community Feeding

Large community meals — Bhandara — are organised at temples. Everyone is fed, regardless of caste or background. This echoes Hanuman's own values: the greatest devotee of Rama, who ate the most simply and served the most greatly. The Bhandara on Hanuman Jayanti is considered especially meritorious — feeding the hungry is Hanuman's seva made real.

Hanuman for the Modern World

Why the most powerful being in Hindu mythology is also the most relatable

Hanuman is not a comforting deity. He is an inspiring one.

He does not promise ease. He shows what becomes possible when strength is combined with complete selflessness. Every quality he embodies — strength, wisdom, courage, devotion, service — is available to every human being who develops it through practice and intention. This is why he resonates across every generation and every background.

💪

Strength Without Ego — The Rarest Combination

Hanuman lifted a mountain. He burned Lanka. He crossed an ocean. He defeated gods in childhood. Yet he describes himself only as Rama's servant. He never sought credit, recognition or reward. In a world obsessed with personal branding and visibility, Hanuman represents the opposite: do the work that needs doing, and let the work speak.
  • For professionals: Competence in service of purpose, not ego. Do excellent work. Don't chase credit.
  • For students: Learn like Hanuman — from any teacher (he learned from the Sun God, moving backwards so as not to disrespect the teacher).
  • For athletes and fitness enthusiasts: Hanuman is the patron of wrestlers, soldiers and martial artists across India. Physical strength developed in his name for 2,000 years.
🏔️

Carry the Mountain — Hanuman's Lesson for Impossible Problems

When Hanuman could not identify the Sanjeevani herb, he did not return empty-handed or pretend he had tried his best. He lifted the entire mountain. This single story is the most practical lesson Hanuman teaches: when you cannot find the specific solution, bring more than was asked for. When the problem is too complex, expand what you're willing to do.
  • In your work: When you can't solve the specific problem — bring the whole mountain. Exceed what was asked.
  • In creative work: When inspiration fails — don't wait for the right idea. Do more, generate more, carry more until the answer reveals itself.
  • For Ansh and music producers: When a track isn't working — don't keep fixing the same element. Change the whole approach.
🫀

Hanuman and Grief — The Deity Who Understands Waiting

Before Hanuman met Rama, he spent years in exile with Sugreeva, serving faithfully with no sign of purpose arriving. He did not know when Rama would come, or if his skills would ever be needed. He waited with faith and continued to grow. For anyone in a period of waiting — searching for work, for the right relationship, for a breakthrough — Hanuman shows that the waiting period is not wasted time. It is preparation time.
  • For diaspora families: Hanuman moved between two worlds — monkey and human, servant and equal — never entirely at home in either. He is the patron of those who live between cultures.
  • For families abroad: On Hanuman Jayanti, light a lamp at sunrise wherever you are in the world. That moment connects you to 100 million others doing the same thing. You are never alone when Hanuman's name is spoken.
