What is Akshaya Tritiya?

One of only four self-auspicious days in the entire Hindu calendar

Akshaya Tritiya is the rarest kind of day — one that needs no astrologer, no muhurat calculation, no consultation. The entire day is auspicious.

The Hindu calendar is governed by an intricate system of auspicious and inauspicious timings — every action ideally performed in the right muhurat. But four days each year are called Abujha Muhurat (self-auspicious, requiring no calculation). Akshaya Tritiya is the most celebrated of them. On this day, the Sun and Moon are simultaneously in their exaltation signs — the Sun in Aries, the Moon in Taurus — a cosmic alignment that happens only once a year. Any charity, puja, marriage, business launch, house purchase or good deed performed today carries eternal fruit. The Sanskrit word Akshaya literally means "that which never diminishes." This is the day of eternal returns.

Why This Day Is Sacred — The Legends

Multiple events from different Yugas converge on this one Tithi

1

The Birth of Treta Yuga — Cosmic New Beginning

According to the Bhavishya Purana, Akshaya Tritiya marks the end of Satya Yuga (the age of perfect truth) and the beginning of Treta Yuga — the era when Rama, Parashurama and other great beings would incarnate. This makes the day a cosmic turning point, not merely a personal auspicious moment. Just as the universe itself began a new cycle on this Tithi, so too can any person, family or venture begin a fresh cycle of their own. The auspiciousness is not just planetary — it is woven into the very fabric of time.
2

Parashurama Jayanti — The Warrior Avatar

Akshaya Tritiya is also the birth anniversary of Parashurama — the sixth avatar of Lord Vishnu, born to sage Jamadagni and Renuka. Parashurama is unique among the Dashavatar: he is a Brahmin who became a warrior, carrying an axe (Parashu) and known for his fierce protection of dharma. When corrupt Kshatriya kings began oppressing the people, Parashurama took it upon himself to restore righteousness — twenty-one times over, according to the Puranas. He is the only avatar who remains alive today — a Chiranjivi, like Hanuman — still meditating in the Mahendra mountains. His presence on this Tithi adds divine protection and righteous energy to any venture begun today.
3

Sudama and Krishna — The Most Beautiful Story of This Day

This is the story that captures the true spiritual heart of Akshaya Tritiya. Sudama was a poor Brahmin — a childhood friend of Lord Krishna in Gurukul. Years later, struggling with poverty, his wife urged him to visit Krishna in Dwarka to ask for help. Sudama went — but he was too ashamed to ask for anything. He brought a small gift: a handful of beaten rice (poha) wrapped in an old cloth, the humblest offering imaginable. When Krishna saw him, he rose from his throne, embraced him, washed his feet, and took the poha and ate it with overwhelming love and joy. Sudama returned home having asked for nothing. But when he arrived, his hut had been replaced by a magnificent palace, his family clothed in gold, his poverty gone forever. The lesson: the universe does not measure the size of your offering. It measures the sincerity of your heart. On Akshaya Tritiya, even the smallest act done with complete love yields eternal abundance.
4

The Akshaya Patra — The Bowl That Never Emptied

During the Pandavas' exile in the forest, they faced a crisis — how to feed the thousands of sages and guests who came to them, when they themselves had nothing? On Akshaya Tritiya, the Sun God Surya appeared to Yudhishthira and gave him a miraculous bowl — the Akshaya Patra — which would produce unlimited food each day, emptying only after Draupadi herself had eaten. This divine gift sustained the Pandavas and all who came to them throughout their twelve years of exile. The Akshaya Patra is the most perfect symbol of what this day represents: abundance that never diminishes, that multiplies with giving, that serves the many not the one.

What to Do on Akshaya Tritiya

In order of spiritual merit — Daan first, gold second

The scriptures are unambiguous: charity is the highest act on this day — not gold purchase.

Gold purchase is the most visible modern tradition, but the ancient texts place Daan (charity), Pitru Tarpan (ancestor remembrance) and Vishnu-Lakshmi puja far above it in spiritual merit. Akshaya Tritiya is a day to give — because everything given today is returned a thousandfold. Gold is a symbol of that prosperity, not its source.

🙏

1. Daan — Charity (Highest Merit)

The Puranas declare that Anna Daan (feeding the hungry) on Akshaya Tritiya carries eternal fruit. Donate food, water, umbrellas, footwear (protective items for the coming summer heat) or money to those in need. In India, the tradition is to give cool water in earthen pots — a beautiful and practical act of summer charity. Any donation today, however small, is believed to return multiplied across many lifetimes. This is the Akshaya — the inexhaustible return.
🌊

2. Pitru Tarpan — Ancestor Remembrance

Offering water and sesame seeds to your ancestors on this day is considered especially meritorious. The Akshaya Tritiya Tithi falls outside the usual Pitru Paksha period but is one of the most auspicious days of the year for tarpan. If there is a river, tank or any flowing water nearby, go there. Otherwise offer water facing south at home. Remember your ancestors by name — this act of remembrance itself is the offering.
🪔

3. Lakshmi-Narayana Puja

Akshaya Tritiya is dedicated to the Lakshmi-Narayana form of Lord Vishnu — Vishnu and Lakshmi worshipped together. Offer yellow flowers, sandalwood paste, incense and a lamp. Chant Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya or the Lakshmi mantra. The combination of Vishnu (sustenance) and Lakshmi (prosperity) invoked on the day when both the Sun and Moon are exalted is considered a particularly powerful alignment for abundance in all its forms.
🚀

4. New Beginnings — Abujha Muhurat

Start that business. Launch that project. Sign the contract. Begin the course. Move into the new home. The entire day requires no additional calculation — whatever begins today carries divine momentum. This is the only day when the traditional requirement to find an auspicious muhurat is waived entirely. Weddings, Griha Pravesh (housewarming), Vidyarambha (first day of study for children), Bhoomi Puja (groundbreaking) — all are considered self-auspicious today.
🥇

5. Gold Purchase — The Popular Tradition

Buying gold on Akshaya Tritiya is one of India's most enduring traditions. Since Vedic times, gold has been considered not merely a metal but a physical manifestation of Goddess Lakshmi — permanent, imperishable, incorruptible. Gold purchased on this day is believed to grow rather than diminish, and to bring Lakshmi's presence into the home. If gold is not accessible, silver, brass, or even barley grains carry the same symbolic meaning. The act of inviting abundance into your life is what matters — not the price tag.

Puja Vidhi

How to perform Akshaya Tritiya puja at home

1
Brahma Muhurta — Wake and Bathe

Wake before sunrise. Apply sesame oil (til ka tel) and bathe — the oil bath on Akshaya Tritiya mirrors the purification of Abhyanga Snan. Mix a few drops of Gangajal into your bath water if available. Wear clean clothes in yellow, gold or white — yellow honours both Vishnu and the exalted Sun.

2
Clean and Prepare the Puja Space

Clean the puja room or altar thoroughly. Sprinkle Gangajal in all corners. Lay a yellow or red cloth on the chowki (altar platform). Place a Kalash (copper pot) filled with water, five mango leaves and a coconut on top — this represents the divine presence invoked into your home.

3
Set Up the Idols

Place the idol or image of Lord Vishnu at the centre. Beside him, Goddess Lakshmi — she is always worshipped with Vishnu on this day. Optionally add Lord Kubera (divine treasurer, guardian of wealth) and Lord Ganesha (remover of obstacles, always worshipped first). Place any gold or silver you intend to purchase before the deities.

4
Offer and Worship

Offer yellow flowers (marigold, champa), sandalwood paste, incense (chandan or kesar agarbatti), a lit lamp (ghee diya) and fruits. Offer Sattu (roasted gram flour), cucumber and gram dal — traditional Akshaya Tritiya bhog. Sprinkle turmeric water on any gold you have placed before the deity.

5
Japa — Chant the Mantra

Chant Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya as many times as possible — 108 is traditional, but even 11 or 27 carries merit. For Lakshmi: Om Shreem Mahalakshmaye Namaha. For Kubera: Om Hreem Shreem Hreem Kuberaya Namaha. The chanting done on Akshaya Tritiya is Akshaya — it does not fade.

6
Aarti and Daan

Conclude with the Lakshmi or Jagdish aarti. Then give — donate food, cool water in an earthen pot, seasonal fruits or money to someone in genuine need. This final act of Dana seals the puja. The prosperity you invite in through worship must flow out through giving — that is the Akshaya cycle.

What Not to Do

Common questions answered

❌

Tamasic Activities

Avoid non-vegetarian food, alcohol, and arguments. This is not superstition — it is the principle that the energy you cultivate on this day is the energy you receive back. A day of clarity, generosity and devotion yields clear and generous returns. A day of dullness and conflict yields the same.
✅

Muhurat — No Calculation Needed

You do not need to consult a Panchang or astrologer for Akshaya Tritiya. The entire day is an Abujha Muhurat — free from all inauspicious influences. Sign that contract. Start that new venture. Move into your home. The cosmos has already cleared the path.

Akshaya Tritiya for the Modern World

Beyond gold — the deeper practice

The Akshaya Principle — What Actually Never Diminishes

Gold is a beautiful symbol but a symbol nonetheless. The deeper teaching of this day is that certain things truly are Akshaya in this world — they don't diminish with use, they grow with giving:

  • Knowledge — shared freely, it multiplies without diminishing in the one who shares it
  • Love — given generously, it does not reduce but expands
  • Good deeds — recorded in the karma of those who give and those who receive
  • A good name — built by right action over time, it outlasts the person
  • Dharma — practiced consistently, it becomes stronger, not weaker
Sudama's Lesson for 2026

The Sudama story is perhaps the most relevant teaching from this day for a world obsessed with calculation and return on investment. Sudama brought his offering with absolutely no expectation of return — he was too ashamed even to ask for help. He gave purely, and received beyond all imagination. The Akshaya return does not come to those who give strategically. It comes to those who give sincerely. On this day, one genuine act of charity, one real prayer, one honest new beginning — carries more weight than a calculated investment in gold.

For Diaspora Families — Starting Something That Lasts

For Hindu families outside India, Akshaya Tritiya offers a beautiful annual ritual: make one meaningful beginning together on this day. Register the children for a new course. Begin a shared family savings goal. Start a habit of weekly Lakshmi puja. Donate to a cause that matters to you. The specific act is less important than the intention — to begin something on the most auspicious day of the year that you intend to sustain. The Akshaya energy is in the commitment to continuity.
